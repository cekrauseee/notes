I’ve made a few portfolios before. I even liked some of them, but there was always something to change before sharing them. A small detail, another idea, a part that could be better. This is the first one I’ve actually put out there.

That’s how personal projects usually go for me. I like coming up with ideas and building things, but I get sidetracked easily. Give me a deadline and I’ll finish. Without one, I can spend all my energy on a tiny part that barely changes the project. By the time I get back to everything else, the excitement is gone.

I thought AI would help with that. I started with autocomplete and gradually changed how I work. These days, I barely write code by hand. I still program, just differently, and I can do a lot more in a lot less time.

The funny thing is, that hasn’t really changed how much I put out into the world. I have more ideas, try more things, and start more projects, but I still leave most of them unfinished.

I think part of it is how easy it’s become to dismiss an idea. I can explore something so quickly that I soon find a reason to drop it. Then another idea comes along, and another. Being faster has also made it easier to give up more often.

At some point, I started looking at everything and wondering what I could build around it. Everything had to become an idea, ideally a product someone would use or buy. I spent all day thinking about making things and somehow still felt like I had no ideas.

Over the past few months, I’ve been trying to take some of that pressure off. Letting an idea sit for a while before deciding whether it’s worth pursuing. Making something because I feel like it, without having to explain where it’s going. Once I managed that, ideas started coming back.

I’m also trying to find a pace I actually enjoy. These are projects I work on in my free time, and I want that time to feel good. I still care about the details. I’m just learning to leave some of them for later.

The first project I put online was aviões. It counts planes and came from an inside joke between my girlfriend and me. It’s a silly little idea that we still use today. It also happens to be something I didn’t spend ages trying to turn into a product I could sell.

Now I’m sharing this portfolio. I’ve put a lot into it, and I like how it turned out, even though I’ll probably find something to change tomorrow. I want it to be a place to write, share thoughts, and show what I’m working on, without waiting for everything to be finished.

I don’t know how often I’ll write here. I still have unfinished projects, and I still get lost in the details. But this portfolio is out, so is this post, and aviões is still part of a joke we share.

Small steps. For now, I’m trying to give them some credit.
